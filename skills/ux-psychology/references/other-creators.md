## 1. Growth.Design (growth.design/case-studies)

- **Format:** Comic-book-style visual "case studies" (not video) — each is an illustrated, panel-based teardown of a specific app's UX, framed as a narrative walking through screens and connecting each UI choice to a named psychology principle. 2–9 min read time per study.
- **Catalog:** 47 case studies as of this search. Apps covered include: Duolingo, Spotify, Instagram, YouTube, Adobe, Amazon, Apple, Tinder, LinkedIn, Signal, Brave, Clubhouse, Headspace, Loom, Trello, Audible, Blinkist, McDonald's, GoDaddy, Uber Eats, Too Good to Go, Grammarly, Airbnb, Hopper, Strava. Organized into categories: Retention, Onboarding, Revenue (and others).
- **Pricing model:** Hybrid — most case studies are free ("Watch now (Free)"); upsells to a paid "UX Course," "UI Course," and a flagship "Product Psychology Masterclass" course (growth.design/course, growth.design/courses/product-psychology). Masterclass markets a case result of one user raising conversion from 11% → 24.7%.
- **Principles named in their case studies (per search):** Zeigarnik effect, loss aversion (streaks/XP), baby schema effect (Duo mascot), labor perception bias, Jobs-To-Be-Done framework, paradox of choice, scarcity, social proof, default bias.
- **Notable NEW items vs. the skill's core list:** **Zeigarnik effect** (incomplete tasks are remembered/nag at users — progress bars, streak counters) and **baby-schema effect** (childlike mascot features trigger protective/positive affect) are not yet in the skill's decision-fatigue/goal-gradient/reciprocity/IKEA/loss-aversion/anchoring list — flag as candidate additions. **Labor perception bias** (making effort visible so users value the output more — e.g., "searching 100 hotels..." loading animations) is also a plausible add, related to but distinct from IKEA/endowment.
- Sources: [growth.design/case-studies](https://growth.design/case-studies), [Duolingo case study](https://growth.design/case-studies/duolingo-user-retention), [Product Hunt listing](https://www.producthunt.com/products/growth-design-case-studies), [Product Psychology Masterclass](https://growth.design/courses/product-psychology)
- Confidence: high on format/catalog (page fetched directly); medium on the exact full principle list (search-summarized, not exhaustive).

## 2. Enrico Tartarotti (YouTube, @enricotartarotti)

- **Angle:** Ex-Product Manager (Amazon, Maze) turned YouTuber/founder. ~210K subscribers. Channel tagline-equivalent: "UX psychology, product thinking, tech culture." Launched Sept 2021.
- **Format:** Long-form video breakdowns of real products' design decisions and psychology — e.g., "Behind The Scenes Of Notion's INSANE Design (w. Notion Team)" (300K+ views), plus videos on Google Maps, ChatGPT, Figma. Emphasis on "how people FEEL about design decisions" rather than pixel-level UI tips — closer to product-strategy storytelling than a rapid screen-by-screen teardown, but still uses before/after and behind-the-scenes screen comparisons.
- **Notable:** Also runs "Storybehind" (storybehind.enricotartarotti.com), a paid breakdown of how his own hit YouTube videos are produced — a meta content-production resource, not UX psychology itself.
- **New principles surfaced:** none specifically named in search snippets (would need to watch videos to confirm) — flag as low confidence on principle content, high confidence on channel identity/existence.
- Sources: [YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@enricotartarotti), [Tubik Studio roundup](https://blog.tubikstudio.com/useful-youtube-channels-for-designers/)

## 3. Rian Dutra / "Design From Human" (YouTube @designfromhuman, Brazil)

- **Angle:** Self-described first YouTube channel + podcast applying Psychology/Cognitive Science/Neuroscience to UX design (Brazilian market, Portuguese-language primarily). Rian Dutra is a cognitive science researcher and author of a best-seller on cognitive biases ("Enviesados") and reportedly released the "first NFT collection on Cognitive Biases in UX Design."
- **Format:** Video + companion Medium/UX Collective essays (e.g., "Design for how people think — an introduction to psychology in UX design") rather than the rapid-fire screen-teardown format of uxpeak; more educational/foundational (explains a bias, then how to apply it) than "here's app X, here's the before/after."
- **New principles surfaced:** Not enumerated in search snippets beyond general "cognitive biases in UX" framing — would need deeper reading of his Medium essays for named biases. Flag low-medium confidence.
- Sources: [Medium announcement](https://designfromhuman.medium.com/the-first-youtube-channel-and-podcast-about-psychology-applied-to-ux-design-a6d931444cc0), [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/designfromhuman), [UX Collective essay](https://uxdesign.cc/design-for-how-people-think-an-introduction-to-psychology-in-ux-design-1ea27fd7b86e)

## 5. Tim Gabe (YouTube channel, timgabe.com — "The Fullstack Creative")

- **Angle:** Design-for-founders creator; helps founders use design to grow (claims: helped raise $40M+, grow DAUs, multiply revenue). Not UX-research-credentialed like the others — more growth/marketing-adjacent.
- **Format:** Video essays specifically themed "Top 1% Apps" — e.g. "The Twisted Psychology Behind Top 1% Apps," "How Top 1% Apps Keep You Hooked," "The Tiny Design Rule Behind Top 1% Apps." This is close in spirit to uxpeak (naming a psychological mechanism behind why elite apps' screens work) though framed around business growth outcomes (retention, fundraising, revenue) rather than pure psychology pedagogy.
- **New principles surfaced:** Search snippets around this "hooked apps" content cluster generally reference Nir Eyal's Hooked Model (Trigger→Action→Variable Reward→Investment), variable rewards/dopamine loops, friction reduction (autoplay, "Next Episode in 5..."), and status/achievement mechanics — these overlap with agent C's habit/engagement-model lane, so flagging but not claiming as "new" for this angle.
- Sources: [Tim Gabe YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC8WLg9gshrtZA1-swL5D9Kg), [timgabe.com](https://timgabe.com/), video titles found via search only (not confirmed by transcript).
- Confidence: medium — channel identity confirmed, but exact principle claims per-video not verified beyond title/snippet.

## 6. Learning Loop (learningloop.io) — not a YouTube channel, but a directly adjacent creator/resource worth flagging

- **Angle:** A structured "Persuasive Patterns" library/playbook (80+ "Plays") explicitly built for product/UX psychology, sold also as a physical card deck and a paid course ("Build Persuasive Products," learningloop.io/courses/product-psychology) plus a book ("The Persuasive Product Psychology" PDF).
- **Format:** Each "play" (e.g., learningloop.io/plays/psychology/endowed-progress-effect, /shaping, /pattern-recognition, /recognition-over-recall, /chunking, /social-proof, /sequencing, /triggers, /framing-effect) is a short page naming the principle, explaining the mechanism, and giving a real product case example (e.g., Last.fm onboarding using Curiosity Effect + Pattern Recognition).
- **Coverage of the core list:** Confirmed pages exist for **Endowed Progress Effect** (IKEA/endowment-adjacent — directly overlaps the "IKEA/endowment effect" bucket) and **Social Proof/Reciprocity/Authority/Scarcity/Consistency** (Cialdini overlap — Agent B's lane). Also surfaces **Framing Effect** and **Chunking/Sequencing/Recognition-over-Recall** (cognitive-load principles, closer to Agent A's Laws-of-UX lane) — worth cross-referencing rather than duplicating.
- **New principle worth flagging:** **Curiosity Effect** (paired with pattern recognition in the Last.fm case) is not in the current core list — candidate addition: the itch to see "how precise/close" a system's predictions/results get, driving repeated small inputs.
- Sources: [learningloop.io/blog/getting-started-with-product-psychology](https://learningloop.io/blog/getting-started-with-product-psychology), [Endowed Progress play](https://learningloop.io/plays/psychology/endowed-progress-effect), [Persuasive Patterns shop deck](https://shop.learningloop.io/products/the-persuasive-product-psychology-pdf-book), [Social Proof play](https://learningloop.io/plays/psychology/social-proof)
